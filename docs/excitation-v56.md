# V56: activity bursts and sleeping eyes

Observed cursor/orb play charges a bounded excitement state. Available metabolic
reserve, sleep pressure, stress, temperament and recovery alter recruitment.
Accepted, measured movement spends charge and increases recovery and sleep need.
Idle time alone does not trigger a burst. Feeding, sleep, protection, dragging and
exclusive object tasks keep their motor ownership.

Five movement families (dash, orbit, zigzag, roll, soft edge reversal) have
continuous phase, intensity, curvature and speed. Native targets go through the
existing acceleration-limited locomotion and liquid solver. No direct particle
impulses or teleporting are added. Edge routes are enabled only on a single
monitor. Existing monitor coverage projection remains authoritative.

Learning uses unique measured interaction windows, with bounded and reversible
cue responses and movement preferences. A held cue is not credited every tick.
Phase and effort advance only when movement is observed. Saved preferences
survive restart; transient motor activity does not replay after absence.

Whole-face roll shares one transform across eyes, brows and mouth. Excitement
modulates the existing slow bounded material tint, never a flashing overlay.
Sleep owns full eye closure; open-eye fill disappears before the closed arcs
fade in. Deliberate unilateral sleep checks remain possible.

This is a virtual-animal control model, not a claim of biological equivalence.
Behavioral references: [AKC on activity bursts](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/why-does-my-puppy-go-wild-at-night/)
and [Cats Protection on overstimulation](https://www.cats.org.uk/cats-blog/why-does-my-cat-bite-me).

Validation: organism persistence, cue reversal, execution gating and frame-rate
tests; native route continuity/ownership tests; body regression suite; production
GPU captures of sleep, tint and whole-face inversion. These checks do not establish
weeks-long behavioral variety; that requires longitudinal observation.
