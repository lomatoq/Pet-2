# V67.1: soft motion, membrane continuity and stable orb light

## Menu

The full edge-to-nest path previously used a symmetric minimum-jerk curve, while opacity finished in roughly 80ms on the compact layout. The combination delayed visible travel then moved opaque controls through the middle of their path quickly. The native window also hid on a wall-clock deadline independent of its last rendered fade.

Travel now uses cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1): the existing long arc settles gradually into place. Its 500ms duration retains the existing distance allowance on wide displays. Opacity has a separate gentle onset, cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.1, 0.25, 1), over 200ms. Closing fades over 250ms with a smaller 12px drift. A reversal starts from the current value; it preserves position/opacity continuity, not velocity continuity. The native window hides only when the rendered dismissal reaches its endpoint and the panel has faded. Reduced motion removes travel and floating but retains the gentle fade.

The [Emil Kowalski motion guidance](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations) informed purposeful spatial motion and restraint; [MDN's easing definition](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/easing-function) supplies the standard `ease` control points. A strong ease-out was rejected for opacity because it increased the first-frame jump. No animation library or new rendering pass was added.

## Membrane

The material carried immutable particle labels directly into a normalized angular light field. Compressed neighborhoods can mix opposing labels; this gave the saved resting creature short disconnected light patches along its floor contact. The new light coordinate follows body-local geometry with a smoothly bounded material displacement. A finite angular core avoids normalizing a near-zero label into a full directional flip. Raw particle-velocity influence on width is smaller and smoothly bounded, and agitation uses a broader, slower wave. Palette, integrated circulation, facial light, and exterior-only backward aberration remain in the existing energy material.

This is a bounded optical coordinate change, not a new simulation. [Valve's flow rendering discussion](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2010/siggraph2010_vlachos_waterflow.pdf) motivates limiting visual distortion; the implementation uses analytic circulation rather than that presentation's texture flow maps.

Native before/after captures hold geometry fixed and perturb material labels by +/-0.006. On the union of bright contour pixels (>65/255), the resting fixture's average RGB response falls from 0.702 to 0.026 code values and its maximum from 10 to 1. The awake fixture's average falls from 0.628 to 0.086. Prescribed smooth compression has a lower resting-frame difference (3.266 to 2.975 mean code values), while the awake difference is effectively unchanged (4.147 to 4.160). This isolates shader sensitivity; it does not prove physical landing smoothness or measure live FPS. Native images show the previous disconnected underside patches becoming a continuous membrane.

The local evidence packet is `reports/v67.1-menu-motion-2026-10-04`: exact before/after binaries, shader copies, raw GPU captures, linear-light menu composites, frame metrics, and replay scripts. `character_material --rim-continuity-only` records the optical oracle. `landing_material` replays recorded particle states at their actual carrier positions with a fixed optical phase, keeping shading and physical trajectories separately testable. The grey floor in its review sheets is explicitly an annotation.

A read-only copy of the current running V67 pet's saved material was also rendered by both shaders. It reproduces abrupt cyan/lilac sectors along the top, sides, and floor. The bounded coordinate produces a continuous membrane on the same saved geometry. Original state files and the running process were not modified.

## Physical landing and reconstruction

The earlier V67 contact fixture moved the root while supplying zero velocity and acceleration; it did not reproduce carrier braking. The replacement replay supplies true incoming velocity, floor collision, delta-v and acceleration. Two different mechanisms delayed the response: a kinematic root stop lost material momentum before the filtered steering acceleration could transfer it, and absolute splat-center smoothing added roughly a100ms time constant to visible deformation.

At a loaded wall the lost incoming normal carrier velocity now reaches main particle mass before the unilateral solve. Measured load is required; a broad-phase plane alone cannot turn airborne braking into a collision. Startup, drag and takeoff do not replay stored momentum. The delayed steering echo is suppressed only after a delivered impulse. A host wall event keeps its existing fallback if the current geometry cannot receive the normal momentum. Detached mass and tangential motion retain their ownership.

Reconstruction centers now travel by their actual solved particle displacement; only the neighborhood centroid residual, covariance, splat axis, aspect and surface score remain filtered. Supported load, final rest targets and material parameters are unchanged.

The host reference contract is explicit. Calling `set_environment_support`, including `None`, permanently enables material-advection reconstruction for that runtime; the capability survives tuning rebuilds and recovery. The production desktop calls this API before every material step, including birth and airborne feeding when no current plane exists. Taking off or switching to an adhesive wall therefore cannot switch reconstruction response. Older somatic-only hosts that never call this API continue their filtered-contour-to-root feedback. An initial unconditional transport broke three legacy supported-settle cases; centroid, coordinate-reframe and viscosity probes did not fix that different feedback contract and were rejected. All four original supported-settle assertions pass unchanged with the compatible contract. A paired fixture supplies the same somatic input and carrier-stop protocol to both owners and verifies loaded response (2.56% legacy versus 3.49% physical height reduction at 100ms/60Hz). Each initial gap is aligned to its own contour; absolute root positions are not identical. The movement catalog explicitly opts into production ownership rather than silently exercising only the legacy branch.

At24px/s, visible height reduction after50/100/200ms changes from0.54/1.25/3.48% to1.45/3.46/8.91%; the1s value remains close (34.38% vs34.80%). At324px/s the100ms response changes from4.00% to14.88%, and support no longer drops then reacquires. New tests exercise30/60/120Hz, near-floor braking, unloaded fallback and takeoff. Controlled replay retains96 main mass, one component, zero failsafe and zero recovery. Existing supported-rest assertions pass with0.0153px contour jitter, below their0.05px bound. These are fixture observations, not universal guarantees about every desktop interaction.

Physical evidence and ablations: `reports/v67-contact-followup-2026-10-05/RESEARCH.md`, `metrics.csv`, copied executables, source hashes and canonical snapshots. The full application gates and exact final-source replay are recorded separately at delivery.

## Orb light over time

The orb shader rotated its light field by `time * 0.095 * (1-r*r)`. This is differential rotation: the radial angular slope grew without bound with uptime. Broad light volumes became subpixel concentric rings and a moire mosaic. Production rotation from throws was already bounded; throws exposed the aging material rather than causing the accumulation.

Age now rotates the whole field rigidly. A separate bounded sinusoidal differential drift preserves flowing light without winding it ever finer. Its radial angular slope stays below 1.36 radians per unit radius regardless of uptime. This changes no physical orb state, sprite, instance layout or rendering pass.

`body_lab --birth-captures <directory> --orb-regression-only --orb-regression-verify` renders 40 native GPU fixtures at 28px and 112px diameters, with production instance packing after 0/1/5/20 deterministic throw-position replays. Optical clocks cover 15s, 10min, 1h and matched phases after 1 and 48 common cycles (about 16.75h). It is an optical regression, not a physical throw simulation. The former shader reproduces the reported circular mosaic at 1h even with zero throws. Across 16 matched-phase comparisons, its worst mean RGB difference is 38.922/255 and maximum 177/255. The bounded field reduces these to 0.056/255 and 3/255, passing the existing fixture tolerances of 0.35/255 mean and 8/255 maximum. Final release captures are recorded separately in the delivery evidence packet.
