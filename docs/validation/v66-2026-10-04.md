# V66: soft spectral ink and behavioral continuity

The accepted visual direction is an opaque near-black creature, a soft moving spectral boundary, and emissive facial features. It replaces the milky-coat response while preserving the reconstructed optical volume, physical silhouette, facial expressions, blink geometry, food-contact mouth socket, and tongue geometry. No particle solver or topology limits change in this release.

The boundary uses contour distance and persistent material coordinates. Broad overlapping fields vary its width, chroma, and local light intensity continuously; local relative material velocity modulates thickness. White light transitions to colored outer envelopes, with a support fade preventing a hard edge at the end of particle kernels. Eyes, lips and brows share slow spatial light flow without losing their expression masks. A compile-time branch excludes the incumbent coat from the active fragment path. No new full-screen passes, textures or raymarch loops are introduced.

The motion wake still uses the existing physical velocity filter. Its exterior-only integral transports coverage rearward, never facial RGB. Native matched captures at rest, slow/right/down/reversal/stop verify visible rear energy, no changed opaque-body or face pixels, and no changed pixels ahead of the optical support. Color evolution in a fixed pose is measured separately from physical motion.

Buttons use a lighter cyan/lilac illustrated material. Six authored vector symbols replace font-atlas glyphs and their dark shadow: rounded 1.7-point strokes with a faint 0.35-point fringe. Owner-scoped care commands, hit regions, semicircle layout and floating timing remain unchanged. Native 1x/2x captures include light/dark/checker, pressed/hover/fade, and fractional positions.

Capsule, seed, nest and orb receive a related ink palette without changing their atlas alpha, layer ownership, apertures or physical rolling coordinates. An opening-frame ablation distinguished a neutral-mist white point from the surrounding pale wash: the wash was the light desktop visible through a creature whose late fade was only 0.107 at the already-open aperture. A shared production/capture visibility curve now prepares the opaque body underneath the revealing seed. Broad aura footprints and neutral mist receive bounded light budgets; saturated particle lights retain their paths and timing.

Behavior now distinguishes appetitive need from current capacity. A serde-default activity state integrates observed rest, effort and safe contact; short aftereffects influence existing affect targets, action costs and expression. Missing sensor evidence cannot fabricate awakening, and a restored transient rest latch cannot do so either. Fatigue affects play/exploration readiness. Protective responses retain priority. This is a bounded engineering model, not a claim to reproduce biological nervous systems.

Validation: workspace all-targets 1,133 passed, zero failed, eight ignored; strict workspace Clippy passed. An explicit normally ignored native GPU test for both button target encodings also passed. After the final optical-support/glow edits, eight shader-related tests passed. Native release captures and package hashes are recorded in the local delivery report. The behavior recorder covers eight paired contexts across three seeds; its measured inputs are fixtures, not a full rendered desktop session. Long-term attachment and subjective naturalness require longitudinal user observation.
